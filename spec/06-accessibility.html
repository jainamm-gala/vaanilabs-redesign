Companion to spec/06-accessibility.md. It shows structure, not styling: where the landmarks are, the order Tab takes, what focus and selection look like in both themes, how the Flow Designer works without a pointer, what a screen reader hears during a call, and how big targets are. Sample data only; no real customers.

2 · Focus is not selection

06 §7.1–7.2 · F §13 · F-A11Y-006, -007

Focus is always a 2 px --focus outline with an offset, drawn on the visible part of the control (never on a hidden 1 × 1 input). Selection is a soft fill with a bar or a 1 px border. When both apply, both show. The same specimens render in the light and dark themes; --focus is ≥ 6.15:1 on every plane.

3 · Flow Designer without a pointer

06 §9.6–9.8, §16, §19.B · FD §16 · F-A11Y-001, -007, -027, -028 · F-FLOW-020

The canvas is one Tab stop; steps are visited in graph order (the step numbers). ↓ moves into a Logic step's answer sockets; Enter, Space or C on a socket opens Connect to…. Every step and connection is named from labels, never ids. The Outline is a complete, non-spatial editor of the same flow.

4 · One call: what is announced, and what is not

06 §12 · N §12.1, §12.4 · CK §4.5 · F-A11Y-014, -022

State changes are read once, politely, after a 500 ms debounce. Only final transcript turns are read, at most one every 2 s, in the language they were spoken (lang on each turn). Timers, cost so far and partial turns are never announced. The transcript is a plain list, not role="log", so interim words do not flood the reader.

5 · Target sizes and pointer alternatives

06 §15–16 · F §14 · C §1.4 · F-A11Y-023, F-A11Y-001

The shaded box is the hit area: at least 24 × 24 on fine pointers and 44 × 44 on touch, padded with ::after so the visual can stay small. No layout shrinks a touch target: the nav’s short mode is fine-pointer only, and canvas answer rows grow to 44 so a socket’s target is the end of its row. Zoomed out, Connect to…, Go to and the Outline are the equivalent paths. Destructive controls keep 8 px from routine ones or live in ⋯. Every drag has a click or select path.
